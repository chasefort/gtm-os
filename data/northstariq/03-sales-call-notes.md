Document control:
- Document type: Discovery call summary
- Status: Approved for internal planning

- Audience: Leadership, sales, product marketing, revenue operations

## Summary

Recent discovery calls suggest enterprise banks and regional financial institutions may have more urgent pain than the original founder memo assumed. The strongest pain is not content creation volume. It is inconsistent field language across branches, relationship managers, advisors, partner channels, and local-market campaigns.

The calls also surfaced a pricing mismatch. Buyers reacted better to a scoped annual pilot tied to one line of business than to a $999/month self-serve entry plan.

## Pipeline Snapshot

### Prospect A: Top-40 Regional Bank

Participants:
- Compliance Operations Lead.
- VP Marketing.
- Retail Banking Enablement Manager.

Primary pain:
- Branch managers adapt campaign language manually.
- Relationship managers reuse old one-pagers.
- Compliance receives late-stage review requests with no source trail.

Direct quote:
> "We already have ChatGPT experiments. The issue is nobody trusts the output enough to send it to the field."

What they asked for:
- Citation trails.
- Approved-claim libraries.
- Restricted phrase flags.
- Comparison against brand and legal guidance.
- Exportable review packets for compliance.

Buying trigger:
- New branch campaign calendar with local-market personalization.

### Prospect B: Wealth Management Division

Participants:
- Advisor Marketing Director.
- Compliance Reviewer.
- Digital Enablement Lead.

Primary pain:
- Advisors want timely social and email content.
- Central marketing cannot create enough approved variations.
- Compliance worries advisors will improvise performance or suitability claims.

What resonated:
- Advisor-safe content blocks.
- Human approval retained.
- Prompt packs for approved language.
- Review trail for generated posts.

Concern:
- "Our advisors will not adopt another tool."

Likely pilot:
- 50 advisors in one region.
- Weekly social post package.
- Compliance approval workflow around approved claims and restricted phrases.

### Prospect C: Enterprise Banking Group

Participants:
- Head of Revenue Enablement.
- Product Marketing Lead.
- Risk Transformation Sponsor.

Primary pain:
- Sellers use inconsistent language for treasury, lending, and digital banking.
- Product marketing updates decks but field teams keep using older versions.
- Competitor responses differ by region.

What resonated:
- Sales battlecards with citations.
- Alignment checks that flag pricing or claim drift.
- Executive-ready source-of-truth summary.

Concern:
- Integrations matter. Their stack includes Salesforce, Seismic, SharePoint, Slack, and Microsoft Teams.

## Buying Committee

Likely champions:
- Revenue Enablement.
- Product Marketing.
- Compliance Operations.

Likely blockers:
- Legal if claims imply automation.
- IT if integrations are unclear.
- Field leadership if workflow adds steps.
- Procurement if priced as generic AI software.

Likely economic buyer:
- VP Marketing, Head of Enablement, or Risk/Compliance Transformation depending on the pilot wedge.

## Messaging That Worked

Strong:
- "Reduce review cycles while keeping compliance in control."
- "Show your source trail before content reaches the field."
- "Reusable approved language for branches, advisors, and relationship managers."
- "A source of truth that sales, marketing, and compliance can inspect."

Weak:
- "AI writing assistant."
- "Content automation."
- "Automated compliance approval."
- "$999/month self-serve."

## Pricing Feedback

Sales should test a $25k annual pilot minimum. Buyers reacted better to a compliance-safe pilot tied to one workflow than to a $999/month self-serve plan.

Recommended pilot structure:
- One line of business.
- 6 to 10 weeks.
- Source library setup.
- Compliance workshop.
- Templates for one workflow.
- ROI measurement plan.
- Pilot success readout.

Promising pilot workflows:
- Retail banking campaign localization.
- Wealth advisor social content.
- Treasury sales objection handling.
- Merger or rebrand language consolidation.

## Urgent Buying Triggers

- New advisor social media program.
- Branch campaign calendar with local-market personalization.
- Merger integration requiring language consistency.
- AI governance initiative from risk or compliance.
- New product launch across distributed relationship managers.
- Regulatory review after field teams used inconsistent language.

## Objections Heard

"We already use ChatGPT."
- Means experimentation exists but governance is missing.

"Compliance will block this."
- Means the buyer needs compliance involved from day one.

"Our advisors will not adopt another tool."
- Means output must be reusable inside existing workflows.

"How do we prove ROI?"
- Means the pilot needs measurable operational impact.

"Can this connect to Salesforce and Seismic?"
- Means integration roadmap needs honest positioning.

## Sales Guidance

Do not lead with self-serve. Lead with a pilot that solves one expensive workflow.

Do not say "automates compliance review." Say "reduces review cycles while keeping compliance in control."

Do not overpromise integrations. Acknowledge the roadmap and explain that the first pilot proves source-grounded output quality before full workflow integration.

Do use the enterprise-bank urgency signal in internal planning, even if the founder memo still names mid-market fintech as the initial wedge.
